Why Are My String of Hearts Leaves Wrinkling?

String of Hearts is loved for its delicate, heart-shaped leaves and long, trailing vines. But when those little hearts begin wrinkling, curling, or losing their usual firmness, it can be difficult to tell whether the plant needs water or something else is wrong. From dry soil and damaged roots to heat and drainage problems, here's how to identify seven possible causes and help your String of Hearts recover.

Root & Room Plant Care · 7 min read
The quick answer

String of Hearts leaves usually wrinkle when they lose more moisture than the plant can replace. Underwatering is a common cause, but damaged roots, overly wet soil, excessive heat, and other growing conditions can also contribute. Before watering, check the soil moisture and overall condition of the plant.

String of Hearts (Ceropegia woodii) is a trailing plant known for its delicate, heart-shaped leaves, slender vines, and small moisture-storing tubers.

Its leaves are somewhat succulent, allowing the plant to store moisture and tolerate short periods of dryness.

When those leaves begin wrinkling, however, it usually means their water balance has been disrupted.

The tricky part is determining whether the plant needs more water or whether something is preventing its roots from absorbing moisture properly.

Start by checking the soil. Wrinkled leaves and dry soil suggest a different problem than wrinkled leaves and consistently wet soil.

1. The plant isn't getting enough water

Underwatering is one of the most common reasons String of Hearts leaves begin looking wrinkled.

Although the plant can store some moisture in its leaves and tubers, those reserves are limited.

When the potting mix remains dry for too long, the plant begins using its stored moisture to maintain essential functions.

As moisture reserves decline, the leaves may become thinner, softer, or visibly wrinkled.

Look more closely if...

  • The potting mix is completely dry
  • Leaves feel thinner or softer than usual
  • The pot feels unusually lightweight
  • Wrinkling appeared after missed waterings
  • Several leaves along the vines look deflated

What to do

Check the moisture level throughout the potting mix. If it has dried out excessively, water the plant thoroughly until excess water drains from the container.

Allow the pot to drain completely, and avoid leaving it standing in collected water.

If the potting mix has become so dry that it repels water, rehydrate it gradually until moisture reaches the root ball.

Going forward, water according to the plant's actual moisture needs rather than following a rigid schedule.

Root & Room Tip

String of Hearts leaves can provide useful clues about moisture levels, but don't rely on their appearance alone. Always check the potting mix before deciding to water.

2. The soil is staying too wet

It may seem surprising, but overwatering can also contribute to wrinkled String of Hearts leaves.

When the potting mix stays saturated for extended periods, oxygen becomes less available to the roots.

This can interfere with normal root function and increase the risk of root rot.

As a result, the plant may struggle to absorb enough water even though the soil remains wet.

Possible signs of excess moisture

  • The potting mix remains wet for many days
  • Leaves wrinkle despite moist soil
  • Some leaves become yellow or translucent
  • Vines begin softening near the soil surface
  • The container has inadequate drainage

What to do

Check the soil moisture before adding more water.

Allow the potting mix to dry appropriately between waterings, and make sure excess water can escape through drainage holes.

Avoid letting the container sit in a saucer filled with water.

If the plant continues declining despite improved watering, investigate the condition of its roots.

Wrinkled leaves don't automatically mean your String of Hearts needs more water.

3. The roots or tubers may be damaged

String of Hearts develops small tubers that help store water and nutrients. Healthy roots and tubers are important for supporting its trailing vines and foliage.

If the root system becomes damaged by prolonged wet conditions, decay, or physical injury, the plant may struggle to absorb moisture.

Leaves can begin wrinkling because the roots are no longer functioning effectively.

Signs to investigate

  • Wrinkling continues despite appropriate watering
  • The soil remains wet unusually long
  • Vines soften or deteriorate near the base
  • Leaves yellow and fall from the vines
  • Roots or tubers appear mushy or decayed

What to do

If your String of Hearts continues declining, carefully remove it from its container and inspect the roots and tubers.

Healthy tubers should feel firm. Tubers that have become mushy or are visibly decaying may indicate rot.

Remove clearly rotted material with clean tools and repot viable portions into a suitable, well-draining potting mix.

Handle the delicate vines and roots gently, since unnecessary disturbance can cause additional stress.

If significant root damage has occurred, healthy vine cuttings may offer another way to preserve the plant.

4. Excessive heat or sunlight is drying out the leaves

String of Hearts generally grows well in bright light, and it can tolerate some gentle direct sunlight.

However, prolonged exposure to intense afternoon sun or excessive heat can increase moisture loss from its delicate leaves.

When the plant loses moisture faster than its roots can replace it, the leaves may become thinner, wrinkled, or dry.

Look more closely if...

  • Leaves wrinkle after exposure to strong sunlight
  • The plant sits against a hot window
  • Some leaves develop dry or scorched patches
  • The potting mix dries unusually quickly
  • Wrinkling is worse on the sunniest side

What to do

Move your String of Hearts away from harsh afternoon sunlight or filter the light with a sheer curtain.

Choose a location with plenty of bright, indirect light and gentle direct sun when appropriate.

Check the soil moisture, since heat may also cause the potting mix to dry faster.

5. Sudden temperature changes are stressing the plant

String of Hearts generally prefers relatively stable indoor temperatures.

Sudden exposure to cold drafts, excessive heat, or abrupt temperature changes can stress the plant.

Severe temperature stress may interfere with normal water balance and contribute to weakened or damaged foliage.

Look more closely if...

  • The plant sits near a drafty window
  • Vines are exposed to heating or cooling vents
  • Wrinkling began after moving the plant
  • Leaves show damage after a cold night
  • The plant experiences frequent temperature swings

What to do

Keep your String of Hearts in a location with relatively consistent temperatures.

Avoid placing it directly beneath air-conditioning vents or beside heating units.

Protect the plant from cold windows during winter and avoid exposing it to sudden temperature extremes.

6. The potting mix isn't allowing proper moisture balance

String of Hearts benefits from a well-draining potting mix that allows air to reach its roots.

Dense or compacted soil can hold excessive moisture, increasing the risk of root problems.

On the other hand, some very dry potting mixes become difficult to rehydrate.

Water may run around the root ball without properly moistening the soil, leaving the plant dehydrated even after watering.

Signs to investigate

  • Water pools on the soil surface
  • The potting mix stays wet for many days
  • Water runs straight through a very dry pot
  • The soil feels dense or compacted
  • Leaves continue wrinkling despite watering

What to do

Check whether water moves through the potting mix evenly and drains freely.

If the mix remains excessively wet or has become severely compacted, consider repotting into a suitable, airy mix.

A well-draining succulent-style mix, adjusted to your growing conditions, can be a useful starting point.

Use a container with drainage holes and avoid choosing a pot much larger than the root system.

Root & Room Tip

Before repotting, check whether the potting mix is actually causing the problem. String of Hearts has delicate vines and roots, so unnecessary disturbance can create additional stress.

7. Pests may be damaging the foliage

Although wrinkled leaves are often associated with moisture problems, pests can sometimes contribute to weakened or distorted foliage.

Mealybugs, spider mites, and other sap-feeding pests may damage String of Hearts leaves and vines.

Significant infestations can interfere with healthy growth and may cause leaves to lose their normal appearance.

Signs to investigate

  • White, cotton-like clusters along the vines
  • Fine webbing around leaves
  • Tiny pale spots or speckling
  • Distorted or damaged new growth
  • Visible insects on the undersides of leaves

What to do

Inspect the leaves, stems, and areas around the nodes carefully.

If pests are present, separate the affected plant from nearby houseplants while identifying the infestation.

Choose an appropriate treatment for the identified pest and follow all product label directions.

Avoid applying pest treatments unnecessarily when no infestation is present.

String of Hearts leaves may regain some firmness when temporary dehydration is corrected. However, severely damaged or dried-out leaves may not fully recover. Healthy new growth is a useful sign that conditions are improving.

Why are YOUR String of Hearts leaves wrinkling?

Wrinkled leaves + completely dry soil

Your String of Hearts may be using up its stored moisture. Check whether the potting mix has dried out excessively, especially if the leaves feel thin or deflated.

Wrinkled leaves + wet soil

Excess moisture may be affecting root function. Check whether the soil remains wet for unusually long periods and whether the container drains properly.

Wrinkling + soft or damaged vines

Root or tuber damage may be preventing the plant from absorbing water. Inspect the base of the vines and check for soft, decaying roots or tubers.

Wrinkling + dry or scorched patches

Excessive sunlight or heat may be increasing moisture loss. Check whether the plant sits against a hot window or receives intense afternoon sunlight.

Wrinkling after a temperature change

Cold drafts, heating vents, or sudden temperature changes may be stressing the plant. Look for recent changes in its environment.

Wrinkling + unusual leaf damage

Inspect the vines and leaves for pests. Look for cotton-like clusters, fine webbing, pale speckling, or distorted new growth.

Can wrinkled String of Hearts leaves recover?

Yes, wrinkled String of Hearts leaves may regain their firmness if the problem is caused by temporary dehydration.

When the plant receives enough water and its roots are healthy, some leaves may gradually become fuller again.

However, leaves that have become severely dried out, damaged, or affected by rot may not recover.

Healthy new growth is often a better sign of recovery than the appearance of older, damaged leaves.

How long does it take wrinkled String of Hearts leaves to recover?

Recovery depends on what caused the wrinkling and how severely the plant has been affected.

If the plant is simply dehydrated and its roots are healthy, some leaves may begin regaining firmness within several days after watering.

Plants with damaged roots or tubers may take much longer to recover.

Severely damaged leaves may remain wrinkled even after growing conditions have improved.

Watch for stable vines, firmer foliage, and healthy new growth rather than expecting an exact recovery time.

Should you water a String of Hearts when its leaves wrinkle?

Not automatically.

Wrinkled leaves often indicate that the plant is losing moisture, but the underlying cause matters.

If the potting mix is very dry, the plant may need water.

However, if the soil remains wet, damaged or poorly functioning roots may be preventing the plant from absorbing moisture properly.

Always check the soil before watering again.

Why are my String of Hearts leaves wrinkled even after watering?

If the leaves remain wrinkled after watering, several explanations are possible.

The potting mix may have become so dry that water is not reaching the roots evenly.

Alternatively, damaged roots or tubers may be unable to absorb enough moisture.

The plant may also need additional time to recover from prolonged dehydration.

Check whether the root ball was properly moistened and whether excess water drained from the container.

If the soil is moist but the plant continues declining, inspect the roots and tubers for possible damage.

Should you remove wrinkled String of Hearts leaves?

Wrinkling alone isn't a reason to remove a leaf.

If the leaf remains green and attached to a healthy vine, it may recover after the underlying problem is corrected.

Removing healthy leaves unnecessarily reduces the plant's ability to photosynthesize.

However, completely dried-out, dead, or decaying leaves can be removed carefully.

Use clean scissors and avoid damaging the delicate vines.

Why are my String of Hearts leaves wrinkling and falling off?

Wrinkling accompanied by leaf drop may indicate more significant stress than mild dehydration alone.

Possible causes include prolonged underwatering, overly wet soil, damaged roots, temperature stress, or pest problems.

Check whether the fallen leaves are dry and shriveled or soft and yellow.

Dry, shriveled leaves may suggest prolonged moisture loss, while soft or yellowing leaves can occur when roots are stressed by excess moisture.

These symptoms are clues rather than definitive diagnoses, so examine the soil and roots as well.

When should you worry about wrinkled String of Hearts leaves?

A few slightly wrinkled leaves may recover after appropriate watering and improved growing conditions.

However, widespread wrinkling, rapid leaf loss, or deteriorating vines deserves closer attention.

Check the potting mix, drainage, lighting, and temperature.

If the soil remains wet while the leaves continue shriveling, inspect the roots and tubers for signs of rot.

If you notice webbing, cotton-like clusters, or unusual leaf damage, examine the plant for pests.

Try to identify the most likely cause before making several care changes at once.
