Plant Library

Peperomia

A compact houseplant with thick, interesting foliage that brings texture and personality to shelves, desks, and windowsills.

Light Bright to medium
Water Let soil dry between watering
Care Moderate attention
Pet safety Generally considered pet-friendly

Before You Buy

Is this plant right for your home?

Peperomias stay relatively compact and come in a wide variety of leaf shapes and textures, making them an easy fit for shelves, desks, and smaller spaces.

Good match if...

You want a smaller houseplant with interesting foliage that won't quickly take over your space.

Think twice if...

You want a large statement plant or prefer plants that can tolerate very low light.

Worth knowing

Peperomia is a large group of plants, so individual varieties can differ in shape, size, and appearance.

Care Basics

Small plant, surprisingly little fuss.

Peperomias generally prefer a little time to dry out between waterings and don't need to be constantly tended.

01

Let the soil dry slightly

Allow the soil to dry somewhat between waterings rather than keeping it consistently damp.

02

Give it bright, indirect light

Bright indirect light can support healthy growth, while harsh direct sunlight may scorch the leaves.

03

Use a pot with drainage

Good drainage helps excess water move away from the roots and makes watering easier to manage.

What Usually Goes Wrong

When a small plant starts looking off, check the roots.

Peperomias store some moisture in their thick leaves and stems, so overly wet soil can become a bigger problem than an occasional missed watering.

01

Yellow or dropping leaves

Overwatering or soil that stays wet for too long can contribute to yellowing and leaf drop.

→ 02

Wilting despite wet soil

If the soil is wet but the plant is still wilting, check the roots for signs of excess moisture.

→ 03

Faded or scorched leaves

Harsh direct sunlight can damage Peperomia leaves, especially when the plant is close to a sunny window.
