BUILD FROM THE GROUND UP

A healthy habitat starts from the ground up.

Every thriving isopod habitat begins with the right materials. From nourishing substrate to moisture-retaining moss and protective leaf litter, each layer plays a role in creating a healthy, balanced environment for your colony.

01 / THE FOUNDATION

Nutrient-Rich Substrate

More than just bedding, substrate provides a place for isopods to burrow, retain moisture, and find nourishment. Organic soil blends enriched with decaying leaves and wood help support a healthy colony.

BEST FOR

Building a nourishing foundation for standalone isopod colonies and bioactive habitats.

KEEP IN MIND

Choose pesticide-free materials without added fertilizers. Substrate depth and moisture requirements vary by species.

02 / MOISTURE & HUMIDITY

Moisture-Retaining Materials

Sphagnum moss and other moisture-retaining materials help create a humid refuge within an isopod enclosure. These areas are especially important because isopods need moisture to breathe and thrive.

BEST FOR

Creating a consistently moist area where isopods can regulate their moisture needs.

KEEP IN MIND

Keep the moss damp, not waterlogged. Most commonly kept species benefit from a moisture gradient, with wetter and drier areas available.

03 / FOOD & SHELTER

Leaf Litter & Natural Cover

Dried leaves, decaying hardwood, and cork bark create a natural forest-floor environment. These materials provide hiding places, support beneficial microorganisms, and give isopods organic matter to break down.

BEST FOR

Providing everyday shelter, natural foraging opportunities, and a steady supply of decomposing organic material.

KEEP IN MIND

Choose pesticide-free hardwood leaves and untreated cork bark. Replenish leaf litter as your colony consumes it, and avoid collecting materials from potentially contaminated areas.

BEFORE YOU BUY

It's what's inside that matters.

A thriving isopod habitat is built from the ground up. Choosing safe, nourishing materials helps your colony find food, shelter, and the moisture it needs to thrive.

01

Start With Living Substrate

Choose an organic, pesticide-free substrate that supports burrowing and decomposition. A blend of suitable soil, decaying hardwood, and leaf litter can provide both structure and nourishment. Avoid mixes with added fertilizers or chemical pesticides.

02

Create a Moisture Gradient

Sphagnum moss can help maintain a consistently moist refuge. For many commonly kept species, keeping one area damp and another comparatively dry allows isopods to choose the conditions they need. Adjust moisture to your species.

03

Provide Food & Natural Cover

A generous layer of pesticide-free hardwood leaf litter provides shelter and an ongoing food source. Cork bark offers hiding places, while suitable decaying hardwood adds organic matter for the colony to break down.

04

Don't Forget Calcium

Isopods need access to calcium to build and maintain their exoskeletons. Cuttlebone, crushed oyster shell, or other appropriate calcium sources can be offered alongside their regular diet.
