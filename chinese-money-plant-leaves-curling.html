Why Are My Chinese Money Plant Leaves Curling?

Chinese Money Plants are loved for their cheerful, coin-shaped leaves. But when those normally rounded leaves begin curling upward, cupping inward, or bending downward, it's natural to wonder what's happening. From watering and light to temperature and root health, here's how to identify seven possible causes and help your Pilea recover.

Root & Room Plant Care · 7 min read
The quick answer

Chinese Money Plant leaves may curl because of inconsistent watering, excessive sunlight, temperature stress, root problems, low humidity, or pests. Some variation in leaf shape is also normal, especially as new leaves develop. Whether your Pilea leaves curl upward or downward, check the soil moisture, light, and overall plant health before changing your care routine.

Chinese Money Plants, also known as Pilea peperomioides, are loved for their distinctive round leaves and playful appearance. But when those coin-shaped leaves begin curling, cupping, or bending in unusual directions, it can be difficult to know why.

The key is understanding whether the curling is affecting new or older leaves, whether the soil is too wet or too dry, and what conditions the plant is growing in.

1. The soil is drying out too much

Chinese Money Plants generally prefer their potting mix to dry slightly between waterings, but prolonged dryness can cause stress.

When the roots cannot access enough moisture, leaves may begin curling, drooping, or losing some of their usual firmness.

Look more closely if...

  • The potting mix feels very dry
  • Leaves are curling and drooping
  • The pot feels unusually lightweight
  • Lower leaves are becoming limp
  • Curling appeared after a missed watering

What to do

Check the moisture below the soil surface. If the potting mix has become excessively dry, water thoroughly until excess water drains from the bottom.

Going forward, allow the upper portion of the potting mix to dry slightly between waterings, rather than keeping it constantly wet or allowing it to remain completely dry for long periods.

2. The roots are staying too wet

Although Chinese Money Plants need regular watering, their roots can struggle when the potting mix remains saturated.

Excessively wet soil reduces oxygen around the roots and can encourage root deterioration. Damaged roots may struggle to absorb water properly, sometimes causing leaves to curl or droop.

Possible root trouble

  • The soil remains wet for many days
  • Leaves curl despite moist soil
  • Lower leaves are yellowing
  • The plant appears wilted or weak
  • Roots are soft, mushy, or deteriorated

What to do

Check that your container has drainage holes and that excess water can escape freely.

Avoid leaving the pot standing in collected water. If the plant continues declining despite appropriate watering, carefully inspect the roots for damage.

Don't automatically add more water to a curling Pilea without checking the soil first.

3. The plant is receiving too much sunlight

Chinese Money Plants generally grow best in bright, indirect light. Strong direct sunlight, particularly during the hottest part of the day, can stress their foliage.

Excessive light and heat may contribute to curling, faded leaf color, or brown patches on exposed foliage.

Root & Room Tip

If your Pilea leaves curl most noticeably during periods of strong sunlight, check whether the plant is receiving too much direct exposure.

What to do

Move your Chinese Money Plant away from harsh afternoon sunlight or use a sheer curtain to filter the light.

Avoid placing it in a very dark location. Bright, indirect light generally supports healthy growth without exposing the leaves to excessive heat.

4. Temperature changes are stressing the plant

Chinese Money Plants generally prefer relatively stable indoor temperatures. Sudden changes, cold drafts, and exposure to excessive heat can stress their foliage.

Leaves may curl or droop when environmental conditions change abruptly, particularly if the plant is placed near an exterior door, cold window, or heating vent.

Look more closely if...

  • Curling began after moving the plant
  • The plant sits near a drafty window
  • Leaves are exposed to hot or cold airflow
  • Symptoms appeared after a sudden temperature change

What to do

Move your Pilea to a location with relatively stable temperatures. Keep it away from cold drafts, heating vents, and areas with extreme temperature fluctuations.

Avoid changing several parts of your care routine at once. Give the plant time to adjust while monitoring new growth.

5. The indoor air is excessively dry

Chinese Money Plants can adapt to typical household humidity, but extremely dry indoor air may contribute to moisture stress and leaf curling.

Heating systems and direct airflow can increase water loss from the foliage, particularly when the soil is also becoming too dry.

Root & Room Tip

Unlike some humidity-loving tropical houseplants, Chinese Money Plants usually don't require unusually high humidity. Check the soil moisture, lighting, and temperature before assuming dry air is causing the curling.

If your home is exceptionally dry, moving the plant away from heating vents may help. A humidifier can also maintain more consistent indoor humidity when needed.

6. Pests may be affecting the leaves

Spider mites, thrips, and other common houseplant pests can damage Chinese Money Plant foliage.

Feeding damage may cause pale speckling, discoloration, distorted new growth, or curling leaves.

Spider mites can be particularly troublesome in warm, dry indoor conditions. Fine webbing may become visible as an infestation develops.

Signs to investigate

  • Tiny pale or yellow speckles on leaves
  • Fine webbing beneath the foliage
  • Distorted or damaged new leaves
  • Visible insects or moving pests
  • Curling accompanied by unusual discoloration

What to do

Inspect both sides of the leaves, especially the undersides and areas around new growth.

If pests are present, separate the affected plant from nearby houseplants while identifying the problem.

Choose a treatment appropriate for the identified pest and follow the product label. Avoid applying pesticides unnecessarily when no pests are present.

7. Some variation in leaf shape is natural

Not every curved Chinese Money Plant leaf indicates a problem. Some leaves naturally develop a slightly cupped or curved appearance.

Young leaves may also look different as they expand and mature.

If your Pilea has firm, healthy-looking foliage, consistent growth, and no signs of discoloration or decline, mild leaf curvature may simply be part of its appearance.

However, sudden or severe curling across several leaves deserves closer attention, especially when accompanied by yellowing, wilting, or damaged new growth.

The direction of the curl alone cannot reliably identify the cause. Check the entire plant and its growing conditions.

Why are YOUR Chinese Money Plant leaves curling?

Curling leaves + very dry soil

Your Chinese Money Plant may be struggling with insufficient moisture. Check whether the potting mix has dried out excessively.

Curling leaves + soggy soil

Excess moisture may be affecting the roots. Check drainage and inspect for root damage if the plant continues declining.

Curling leaves + faded patches

Strong direct sunlight may be stressing the foliage, especially if affected leaves face a bright window.

Curling after moving the plant

Changes in temperature, light, or watering conditions may be responsible. Check for drafts and sudden environmental changes.

Curling leaves + pale speckles

Spider mites, thrips, or other pests may be affecting the foliage. Inspect the undersides of leaves for insects and feeding damage.

Slightly cupped, healthy leaves

Mild curvature can be normal. If the foliage remains firm, growth is healthy, and no other symptoms appear, there may be nothing to correct.

Why are my Chinese Money Plant leaves curling upward?

Upward curling can occur when your Chinese Money Plant is experiencing moisture stress, excessive sunlight, or sudden environmental changes.

However, some Pilea leaves naturally develop a slightly cupped appearance, even when the plant is healthy.

Check the soil moisture, lighting, and overall foliage before assuming something is wrong.

Why are my Chinese Money Plant leaves curling downward?

Downward curling may be associated with underwatering, excessively wet soil, temperature stress, or root problems.

The direction of the curl alone cannot reliably identify the cause.

If the soil is very dry, investigate underwatering. If it remains consistently wet and the plant is declining, check drainage and root health.

Can curled Chinese Money Plant leaves uncurl?

Sometimes. Leaves that curl because of temporary stress may regain a more typical appearance once growing conditions improve.

However, leaves that have developed permanent damage or unusual shapes during growth may not completely flatten.

Healthy new growth is often a better sign of recovery than the appearance of older leaves.

How long does it take Chinese Money Plant leaves to uncurl?

Recovery depends on what caused the curling and how severely the plant was affected.

Leaves affected by temporary underwatering may begin improving within several hours to a few days after proper watering.

If curling involves root damage, pests, or prolonged environmental stress, improvement may take longer.

Some leaves may never completely return to their previous shape.

Focus on stable foliage and healthy new growth rather than expecting an exact recovery timeline.

Should you cut off curled Chinese Money Plant leaves?

Curling alone isn't a reason to remove a leaf.

If the leaf remains green, firm, and otherwise healthy, leave it attached while you address the underlying growing conditions.

If a leaf becomes completely brown, dried out, or severely damaged, you can remove it using clean, sharp scissors.

Avoid removing large amounts of healthy foliage unnecessarily.

When should you worry about curling Chinese Money Plant leaves?

Mild curvature on otherwise healthy leaves is not necessarily a problem.

However, sudden or widespread curling deserves attention, especially when accompanied by yellowing, wilting, discoloration, or distorted new growth.

Check the soil moisture, drainage, light exposure, and temperature.

Inspect the undersides of leaves for pests if you notice unusual speckling, webbing, or feeding damage.

Avoid changing several parts of your care routine at once. Identify the most likely cause first, then monitor how the plant responds.

A little observation can help you identify the problem while avoiding unnecessary adjustments.
