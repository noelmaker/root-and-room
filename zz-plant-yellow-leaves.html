Why Are My ZZ Plant Leaves Turning Yellow?

ZZ Plants are known for their glossy green leaves, sturdy upright stems, and ability to tolerate less-than-perfect growing conditions. But when their leaves begin turning yellow, it can be difficult to tell whether the problem is too much water, too little water, or something happening beneath the soil. From root health and lighting to temperature stress and natural aging, here's how to identify seven possible causes and help your ZZ Plant recover.

Root & Room Plant Care · 7 min read
The quick answer

ZZ Plant leaves commonly turn yellow because of overwatering, but other causes include underwatering, damaged roots or rhizomes, excessive sunlight, temperature stress, natural leaf aging, and pests. Before changing your watering routine, check the soil moisture, drainage, and overall plant health.

ZZ Plants (Zamioculcas zamiifolia) are popular houseplants known for their glossy foliage, upright growth, and ability to tolerate occasional neglect.

One reason they're relatively drought-tolerant is their underground rhizomes, which store water and help the plant survive periods without frequent watering.

However, these moisture-storing structures also make ZZ Plants vulnerable to problems when their potting mix remains excessively wet.

When leaves begin turning yellow, the plant may be responding to watering problems, environmental stress, or natural aging.

Start by checking the soil and identifying which leaves are yellowing. A single older yellow leaf can mean something very different from widespread yellowing.

1. The soil is staying too wet

Overwatering is one of the most common reasons ZZ Plant leaves begin turning yellow.

ZZ Plants store moisture in their rhizomes and can tolerate periods of dry soil.

When the potting mix stays saturated for extended periods, oxygen becomes less available to the roots.

This can interfere with normal root function and increase the risk of root and rhizome rot.

As the plant becomes stressed, leaves may begin yellowing, sometimes accompanied by soft stems or leaf drop.

Look more closely if...

  • The potting mix stays wet for many days
  • Several leaves are turning yellow
  • Stems feel unusually soft
  • The pot has inadequate drainage
  • Yellowing began after frequent watering

What to do

Check the soil moisture before watering again.

Allow the potting mix to dry appropriately between waterings, and make sure excess water can drain freely from the pot.

Avoid leaving the container standing in collected water.

If the soil remains wet for unusually long periods, investigate whether the potting mix or container is retaining too much moisture.

Root & Room Tip

ZZ Plants generally tolerate dry soil better than constantly wet conditions. Don't water simply because several days have passed. Check the potting mix first.

2. The plant isn't getting enough water

Although ZZ Plants are drought-tolerant, they still need occasional watering to maintain healthy foliage and growth.

When the potting mix remains excessively dry for a prolonged period, the plant may begin using its stored moisture.

Severe or extended dehydration can eventually cause leaves to yellow, dry out, or drop.

Possible signs of underwatering

  • The potting mix is extremely dry
  • The pot feels unusually lightweight
  • Some leaves develop dry or crispy edges
  • Leaves appear less firm than usual
  • The plant has gone a long time without water

What to do

Check the moisture level throughout the potting mix.

If the soil is excessively dry, water thoroughly until excess water drains from the bottom of the pot.

If the potting mix has become difficult to rehydrate, moisten it gradually so water reaches the root ball.

Allow excess water to drain completely before returning the plant to its usual location.

Going forward, adjust watering according to the plant's growing conditions rather than following a rigid schedule.

3. The roots or rhizomes may be damaged

ZZ Plants grow from underground rhizomes that store moisture and support their root systems.

Healthy roots and rhizomes are essential for absorbing water and maintaining healthy foliage.

When roots become damaged by prolonged saturation, decay, or physical injury, they may struggle to supply enough water and nutrients to the leaves.

Damaged or rotting rhizomes can also interfere with the plant's ability to store moisture.

Yellowing leaves, declining growth, and soft stems may develop when these underground structures are no longer healthy.

Signs to investigate

  • Yellowing continues despite improved watering
  • Stems become soft or mushy near the base
  • The potting mix stays wet unusually long
  • Roots appear mushy or deteriorated
  • Rhizomes feel soft instead of firm

What to do

If your ZZ Plant continues declining, carefully remove it from its pot and inspect the roots and rhizomes.

Healthy rhizomes should feel firm. Soft, mushy, or visibly decaying rhizomes may indicate rot.

Remove clearly rotted material using clean tools, preserving healthy roots and rhizomes whenever possible.

Repot viable portions into a suitable, well-draining potting mix and a container with drainage holes.

Avoid using an excessively large pot, which may retain more moisture than the remaining root system can use.

If significant root or rhizome damage has occurred, recovery may be slow and some yellow leaves may not recover.

4. The plant is receiving too much direct sunlight

ZZ Plants are known for tolerating lower-light environments, but they generally grow well in bright, indirect light.

Although they can tolerate some gentle direct sunlight, prolonged exposure to intense sunlight may damage their foliage.

Excessive sunlight can cause leaves to become pale, yellow, or scorched, particularly when the plant is suddenly moved into a brighter location.

Look more closely if...

  • Yellowing is concentrated on sun-exposed leaves
  • Leaves develop pale or bleached patches
  • Brown, dry areas appear on the foliage
  • The plant sits against a hot, sunny window
  • Yellowing began after moving the plant

What to do

Move your ZZ Plant away from harsh direct sunlight or filter the light using a sheer curtain.

Choose a location with bright, indirect light when possible.

If moving the plant into a brighter location, introduce the change gradually to reduce the risk of leaf damage.

Leaves that have already developed severe sun damage may not return to their original appearance.

5. Temperature changes are stressing the plant

ZZ Plants generally prefer relatively stable indoor temperatures.

Sudden exposure to cold drafts, excessive heat, or abrupt temperature changes can stress the plant.

Significant temperature stress may damage foliage and contribute to yellowing or leaf drop.

Look more closely if...

  • The plant sits near a drafty window
  • Leaves are exposed to heating or cooling vents
  • Yellowing began after a sudden temperature change
  • The plant was recently moved outdoors or indoors
  • Leaves show damage after exposure to cold

What to do

Keep your ZZ Plant in a location with relatively consistent indoor temperatures.

Move it away from cold drafts, heating vents, and direct air-conditioning airflow.

Avoid exposing the plant to sudden temperature extremes.

If the plant was recently relocated, monitor its condition while it adjusts to the new environment.

6. Older leaves are naturally aging

Not every yellow ZZ Plant leaf indicates a serious growing problem.

As ZZ Plants mature, individual older leaves may naturally yellow and eventually fall.

This process is part of normal plant aging and does not necessarily mean the plant is unhealthy.

However, widespread yellowing or rapid leaf loss is less likely to be explained by natural aging alone.

Signs of possible natural aging

  • Only one or two older leaves are yellowing
  • The rest of the foliage remains healthy
  • Stems are firm and upright
  • The plant shows no signs of rot or pests
  • Yellowing develops gradually rather than suddenly

What to do

If only a small number of older leaves are yellowing and the rest of the plant looks healthy, continue monitoring it.

Maintain appropriate watering, lighting, and drainage.

Once a leaf has completely yellowed or dried, you can remove it carefully using clean scissors.

Root & Room Tip

Pay attention to the pattern of yellowing. One older yellow leaf on an otherwise healthy ZZ Plant is less concerning than several stems yellowing at once.

7. Pests may be damaging the foliage

ZZ Plants are generally resilient houseplants, but they can occasionally develop pest problems.

Sap-feeding pests, including mealybugs, scale insects, and spider mites, may damage the foliage.

Significant infestations can contribute to yellowing, discoloration, or weakened growth.

Some pests hide along stems, beneath leaves, or near the points where leaflets attach.

Signs to investigate

  • White, cotton-like clusters on stems or leaves
  • Small, raised bumps attached to plant surfaces
  • Fine webbing or pale speckling on foliage
  • Sticky residue on leaves or nearby surfaces
  • Yellowing accompanied by visible insects

What to do

Carefully inspect the leaves, stems, and areas around new growth.

If pests are present, separate the affected ZZ Plant from nearby houseplants while identifying the problem.

Choose an appropriate treatment for the identified pest and follow all product label directions.

Avoid applying pest treatments unnecessarily when no infestation is present.

Yellow ZZ Plant leaves generally won't turn green again once significant yellowing has occurred. However, correcting the underlying problem can help protect healthy foliage and support future growth.

Why are YOUR ZZ Plant leaves turning yellow?

Yellow leaves + consistently wet soil

Overwatering may be stressing the roots. Check whether the potting mix stays wet for unusually long periods and whether the container has adequate drainage.

Yellow leaves + extremely dry soil

Prolonged underwatering may be affecting the plant. Check whether the potting mix has dried out excessively, especially if the leaves are becoming dry or brittle.

Yellowing + soft or mushy stems

Root or rhizome rot may be developing. Inspect the base of the stems and check whether the underground rhizomes remain firm and healthy.

Yellowing + pale or scorched patches

Excessive direct sunlight may be damaging the foliage. Check whether the plant receives intense afternoon sunlight or sits directly against a hot window.

One or two older yellow leaves

Natural leaf aging may be responsible, especially when the rest of the plant remains healthy and its stems are firm and upright.

Yellowing + unusual leaf damage

Inspect for pests such as mealybugs, scale insects, or spider mites. Look for cotton-like clusters, raised bumps, pale speckling, or fine webbing.

Can yellow ZZ Plant leaves turn green again?

In most cases, ZZ Plant leaves that have turned significantly yellow will not return to their original green color.

Yellowing often occurs when chlorophyll breaks down because of aging, stress, or damage.

However, correcting the underlying problem can help prevent additional healthy leaves from turning yellow.

Focus on protecting healthy foliage and encouraging new growth rather than trying to restore severely yellowed leaves.

Should you remove yellow leaves from a ZZ Plant?

You don't necessarily need to remove a leaf as soon as it begins turning yellow.

If the leaf is still partially green and otherwise healthy, you can leave it attached while monitoring the plant.

Once a leaf has completely yellowed, dried out, or died, you can remove it carefully using clean scissors.

If an entire stalk has become mushy or is showing signs of rot, investigate the roots and rhizomes rather than simply trimming the foliage.

How can you tell if a ZZ Plant is overwatered?

Overwatered ZZ Plants may develop yellowing leaves, soft stems, or declining growth.

One of the most useful clues is potting mix that remains wet for unusually long periods.

However, yellow leaves alone do not confirm overwatering.

Check the soil moisture, drainage holes, and condition of the stems before making changes.

If the plant continues declining or the stems become mushy, carefully inspect the roots and rhizomes for rot.

How often should you water a ZZ Plant?

ZZ Plants generally prefer their potting mix to dry considerably between waterings.

However, there is no single watering schedule that works for every ZZ Plant.

Watering frequency depends on factors such as light, temperature, pot size, potting mix, and seasonal growth.

A plant growing in bright indirect light may use water more quickly than one growing in a dimmer location.

Check the potting mix before watering and water thoroughly when the plant needs moisture.

Allow excess water to drain completely.

Why are my ZZ Plant leaves turning yellow even though I barely water it?

Infrequent watering does not automatically rule out excess moisture.

A large container, dense potting mix, or poor drainage may allow the soil to remain wet for extended periods.

On the other hand, prolonged underwatering can also cause yellowing and leaf loss.

Other possible causes include root damage, temperature stress, excessive sunlight, pests, or natural leaf aging.

Check the actual soil moisture and overall plant condition rather than relying only on how often you water.

Why are the lower leaves on my ZZ Plant turning yellow?

A small number of older leaves near the base of a ZZ Plant may naturally yellow as they age.

This is generally less concerning when the rest of the plant remains healthy.

However, yellowing lower leaves can also occur when the roots are stressed by excessive moisture or other growing problems.

If several leaves or entire stalks are yellowing, check the potting mix and inspect the stems for softness or decay.

Avoid assuming that all lower leaf yellowing is natural aging.

When should you worry about yellow ZZ Plant leaves?

One or two older yellow leaves on an otherwise healthy ZZ Plant may not be cause for concern.

However, widespread yellowing, rapid leaf loss, or soft and mushy stems deserve closer attention.

Check the soil moisture, drainage, lighting, and temperature.

If the soil remains wet while the plant continues declining, investigate the roots and rhizomes for possible rot.

If you notice unusual spotting, raised bumps, webbing, or sticky residue, inspect the foliage for pests.

Try to identify the most likely cause before making several changes at once.

Healthy new growth, firm stems, and stable green foliage are encouraging signs that your ZZ Plant is doing well.
