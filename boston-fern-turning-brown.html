Why Is My Boston Fern Turning Brown?

Boston Ferns are loved for their lush, feathery fronds, but those beautiful green leaves can quickly become brown, crispy, or thin when growing conditions aren't quite right. From watering problems to dry indoor air, here are seven possible causes of browning and how to help your fern recover.

Root & Room Plant Care · 7 min read
The quick answer

Boston Ferns commonly turn brown because of inconsistent watering, dry indoor air, excessive sunlight, or root problems. Fertilizer buildup, temperature stress, and natural frond aging can also contribute. Check whether the soil is too wet or too dry, where the browning appears, and whether healthy new growth is developing before changing your care routine.

A healthy Boston Fern can create a beautiful cascade of soft, feathery green fronds. But when those fronds begin turning brown, drying out, or dropping their tiny leaflets, it can feel like your plant is falling apart.

The good news is that browning doesn't always mean your fern is dying. The key is understanding whether the fronds are becoming dry and crispy, turning brown while the soil stays wet, or simply aging as new growth develops.

1. The soil is drying out too much

Boston Ferns generally prefer consistently moist, well-draining potting mix. Unlike drought-tolerant houseplants, they can struggle when their roots become excessively dry.

When the potting mix dries out completely, fronds may become brittle, develop brown tips, or shed their small leaflets. Repeated drying can leave the plant looking thin and unhealthy.

Look more closely if...

  • The soil feels very dry below the surface
  • Fronds are crispy or brittle
  • Small leaflets are falling onto the floor
  • The pot feels unusually lightweight
  • Browning follows a missed watering

What to do

Check the moisture in the potting mix. If it has become excessively dry, water thoroughly until excess water drains from the bottom of the container.

Going forward, aim to keep the soil lightly and consistently moist without leaving it saturated. Check moisture regularly rather than relying on a fixed watering schedule.

2. The indoor air is too dry

Boston Ferns appreciate humidity, and very dry indoor conditions can contribute to brown tips, crispy fronds, and leaflet shedding.

Heating systems, air conditioning, and dry seasonal air may make it harder to maintain the lush foliage these ferns are known for.

Root & Room Tip

If your Boston Fern repeatedly develops crispy tips despite appropriate soil moisture, consider checking indoor humidity with a hygrometer. A humidifier can help maintain more consistent conditions.

Keep your fern away from heating vents and strong air currents. Avoid assuming humidity is the problem without checking the soil, since underwatering can produce similar symptoms.

3. The roots are staying too wet

Boston Ferns enjoy moisture, but constantly waterlogged soil can reduce oxygen around their roots and encourage root deterioration.

When roots become damaged, they may struggle to absorb water properly. This can cause fronds to wilt, yellow, or brown even though the potting mix feels wet.

Possible root trouble

  • The potting mix remains saturated for days
  • Fronds wilt despite moist soil
  • Several fronds are yellowing or browning
  • The container has poor drainage
  • Roots appear soft, mushy, or deteriorated

What to do

Allow excess water to drain freely and avoid leaving the pot standing in collected water.

If the plant continues declining despite appropriate watering, inspect the roots and potting mix for signs of deterioration. Healthy roots should generally feel firm rather than mushy.

4. The fern is receiving too much sunlight

Boston Ferns generally prefer bright, indirect light or filtered light. Strong direct sunlight, especially during the hottest part of the day, can damage their delicate fronds.

Sun damage may appear as pale, bleached, or brown patches on foliage exposed to intense light. Affected leaflets can eventually become dry and crispy.

If your fern sits in a sunny window, consider moving it farther from the glass or using a sheer curtain to soften the light.

5. Fertilizer buildup is stressing the roots

Boston Ferns benefit from occasional feeding during active growth, but excessive fertilizer can cause problems.

Soluble salts from fertilizer can accumulate in container soil and interfere with the roots' ability to absorb water. This may contribute to brown tips, damaged leaflets, or declining foliage.

Look more closely if...

  • Browning began after heavy fertilizing
  • Leaf tips appear dry or scorched
  • A white crust has formed on the soil or pot
  • The plant is declining despite appropriate watering

If fertilizer buildup seems likely, pause feeding and review how frequently you're fertilizing. When the roots are healthy and the pot drains properly, flushing the potting mix with clean water may help remove excess soluble salts.

Avoid adding more fertilizer to a stressed fern in hopes of encouraging faster recovery.

6. Temperature changes are causing stress

Boston Ferns generally grow best in relatively stable conditions. Sudden temperature changes, cold drafts, or hot, dry air can stress their foliage.

A fern placed near an exterior door, heating vent, or poorly insulated window may experience repeated environmental changes.

If browning began after moving your plant or during a seasonal temperature change, consider whether its location is contributing to the problem.

Root & Room Tip

Before changing several parts of your care routine, check whether your Boston Fern is exposed to drafts or direct airflow from heating and cooling systems.

7. Older fronds are naturally declining

Not every brown frond indicates a serious plant-care problem. Boston Ferns naturally replace older foliage as they grow.

Some older fronds may gradually yellow, turn brown, and dry out while the plant continues producing healthy new growth.

If only a few older fronds are declining and the rest of the plant remains green and vigorous, natural aging may be responsible.

However, widespread browning, rapid leaflet loss, or damage affecting new growth deserves closer attention.

The most useful sign of recovery is healthy new growth and browning that stops spreading.

Why is YOUR Boston Fern turning brown?

Crispy fronds + very dry soil

Your Boston Fern may be struggling with insufficient moisture. Check whether the potting mix has been drying out completely.

Brown tips + dry indoor air

Low humidity may be contributing to crispy tips, especially if your fern sits near heating or cooling vents.

Brown fronds + constantly wet soil

Check the drainage and inspect the roots if the plant continues declining. Saturated soil can contribute to root damage.

Brown patches + direct sunlight

Sun damage may be responsible if browning appears on fronds exposed to strong sunlight.

Brown tips + recent fertilizing

Excess fertilizer or soluble-salt buildup may be contributing. Review your feeding routine before adding more fertilizer.

A few older fronds turning brown

If the remaining foliage is healthy and new growth is developing normally, natural aging may be responsible.

Should you cut brown leaves off a Boston Fern?

If a Boston Fern frond has become completely brown and dried out, you can remove it using clean, sharp scissors or pruning shears.

Cut the damaged frond near its base without disturbing the healthy growth around it. Removing dead foliage can help keep your fern looking tidy.

However, if only the tips or a few leaflets have turned brown, the remaining green portions can still contribute to the plant's growth. You don't need to remove an entire frond simply because it has a little damage.

Can a brown Boston Fern turn green again?

Once a Boston Fern frond has turned completely brown and the tissue has died, it won't return to its original green color.

However, that doesn't mean your entire fern is beyond recovery.

If the roots and growing points remain healthy, correcting the underlying problem may allow the plant to produce fresh green fronds.

Focus on healthy new growth rather than trying to restore fronds that have already died.

How do you revive a Boston Fern that's turning brown?

The best way to help a struggling Boston Fern recover is to identify what's causing the browning before making major changes.

Start by checking the potting mix. If the soil is extremely dry, water thoroughly and allow excess water to drain away.

If the soil is constantly saturated, check the drainage and inspect the roots if the plant continues to decline.

Next, evaluate the fern's location. Provide bright, indirect light, protect it from harsh afternoon sunlight, and keep it away from heating vents and cold drafts.

If indoor humidity is very low, consider using a humidifier to maintain more consistent growing conditions.

Avoid heavy fertilizing while the plant is stressed. Give your fern time to recover and watch for healthy new fronds.

When should you worry about brown Boston Fern leaves?

A few brown tips or older fronds aren't necessarily cause for concern.

However, widespread browning, rapid leaflet shedding, or fronds wilting despite moist soil deserve closer attention.

Check for root problems if the potting mix stays wet for extended periods. Also inspect the foliage for pests if you notice unusual spotting, webbing, or visible insects.

If new growth is repeatedly becoming damaged or the plant is declining quickly, review its growing conditions rather than assuming the browning is natural.
