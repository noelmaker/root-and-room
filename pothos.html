Plant Library

Pothos

A forgiving trailing houseplant known for fast-growing vines, heart-shaped leaves, and an easygoing nature.

Light Bright to low
Water Let soil dry between watering
Care Very little attention
Pet safety Not pet-friendly

Before You Buy

Is this plant right for your home?

Pothos are adaptable, forgiving, and happy to grow as trailing vines. They're a great starting point for many plant parents, but their growth habit is worth considering.

Good match if...

You want an easygoing plant that can trail from a shelf, climb a support, or fill an empty corner.

Think twice if...

You have pets that may chew on your houseplants or don't want a plant that can become quite vining.

Worth knowing

Pothos can tolerate lower light, but brighter indirect light can help support fuller, more vigorous growth.

Care Basics

Let it grow, then trim it back when you want.

Pothos are forgiving and adaptable, making them one of the easier houseplants to work into your routine.

01

Let the soil dry between waterings

Allow the soil to dry before watering again rather than keeping it consistently damp.

02

Give it bright, indirect light

Pothos can handle lower light, but brighter indirect light generally supports stronger, fuller growth.

03

Trim the vines when needed

Regular pruning can help keep long vines manageable and encourage a fuller-looking plant.

What Usually Goes Wrong

Most pothos problems start with the basics.

When the leaves start looking different, check the soil, light, and roots before assuming something complicated is wrong.

01

Yellow leaves

Yellowing can have several causes, but consistently wet soil is one of the first things to check.

→ 02

Drooping leaves

Check the soil before watering. Both overly dry and overly wet conditions can leave a plant looking unhappy.

→ 03

Long, sparse vines

Low light can contribute to stretched growth and larger gaps between leaves along the vines.
