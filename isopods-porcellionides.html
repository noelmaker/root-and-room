Discover the Porcellionides.

Fast-moving, active, and surprisingly colorful, Porcellionides isopods are familiar favorites in the hobby. Best known for their Powder Blue and Powder Orange varieties, these little decomposers are fascinating to watch and play an important role in bioactive environments.

More than just Powder Blue.

Powder Blue might be the name you recognize first, but it is only one of several appearances found in Porcellionides pruinosus. From bright oranges to pale whites and patterned varieties, explore the different forms kept in the hobby and what sets them apart.

01 / PORCELLIONIDES PRUINOSUS

Powder Blue

Powder Blue isopods are known for their soft blue-gray coloring and slightly dusty appearance. They're quick on their feet, often darting beneath leaves and bark when disturbed. As one of the most familiar forms of Porcellionides pruinosus, they're frequently kept in bioactive enclosures and established isopod colonies.

SPECIES & CHARACTERISTICS
P. pruinosus Blue-Gray Fast-Moving Bioactive Favorite
02 / PORCELLIONIDES PRUINOSUS

Powder Orange

Powder Orange isopods stand out with their vivid orange coloring, making them easy to spot against dark substrate and leaf litter. Like other Porcellionides pruinosus, they're active scavengers that feed on decaying organic material. Their striking appearance and relatively straightforward care have made them a popular choice for both display colonies and bioactive setups.

SPECIES & CHARACTERISTICS
P. pruinosus Bright Orange Active Scavenger Beginner-Friendly
03 / PORCELLIONIDES PRUINOSUS

Oreo Crumble

Oreo Crumble isopods have a distinctive mix of dark and pale markings that gives each individual a slightly different appearance. Some have larger patches of white, while others show more scattered patterns across their bodies. Beneath those contrasting colors, they're the same quick-moving, active decomposers as other Porcellionides pruinosus varieties.

SPECIES & CHARACTERISTICS
P. pruinosus Dark & Pale Mottled Pattern Variable Markings
04 / PORCELLIONIDES PRUINOSUS

Whiteout

Whiteout isopods are known for their pale, nearly white appearance, with little of the darker coloring seen in other Porcellionides pruinosus varieties. Their light bodies stand out against soil, bark, and fallen leaves, making them particularly noticeable in naturalistic enclosures. Despite their unusual coloring, they share the active behavior and general care needs of other P. pruinosus.

SPECIES & CHARACTERISTICS
P. pruinosus Pale White Light Color Morph Active Species
05 / PORCELLIONIDES PRUINOSUS

Orange Cream

Orange Cream isopods combine warm orange coloring with lighter, cream-colored patches. Unlike the more uniform appearance of Powder Orange, their markings can vary from one individual to another. The combination creates a softer, patterned appearance while retaining the familiar body shape and lively behavior of Porcellionides pruinosus.

SPECIES & CHARACTERISTICS
P. pruinosus Orange & Cream Patterned Morph Variable Markings
06 / PORCELLIONIDES PRUINOSUS

Red Koi

Red Koi is a hobby name used for patterned isopods with reddish-orange coloring and contrasting pale areas. The amount and placement of color can vary between individuals, giving the colony a mix of appearances rather than one uniform pattern. As with other P. pruinosus varieties, the name describes its appearance, not a separate scientific species.

SPECIES & CHARACTERISTICS
P. pruinosus Reddish-Orange Koi Pattern Variable Markings
