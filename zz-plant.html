Plant Library

ZZ Plant

A glossy, architectural houseplant known for its slow growth, drought tolerance, and remarkably low-maintenance nature.

Light Bright to low
Water Let soil dry well between watering
Care Very little attention
Pet safety Not pet-friendly

Before You Buy

Is this plant right for your home?

ZZ Plants are known for handling periods of neglect, making them appealing for busy homes and newer plant parents. Their slower growth is part of the deal.

Good match if...

You want a low-maintenance plant that can handle lower light and doesn't need frequent watering.

Think twice if...

You want fast, dramatic growth or need a plant that's considered safe around pets.

Worth knowing

ZZ Plants store water in thick underground rhizomes, which helps them tolerate periods between waterings.

Care Basics

The less you fuss, the happier it may be.

ZZ Plants are naturally good at handling periods of neglect. The biggest favor you can do for one is avoiding too much water.

01

Let the soil dry well

Allow the soil to dry thoroughly between waterings. When in doubt, waiting a little longer is generally better than watering too soon.

02

Keep it out of harsh sun

ZZ Plants can adapt to lower light, while bright indirect light can support healthy growth.

03

Don't water on a schedule

Check the soil rather than watering simply because a certain number of days have passed.

What Usually Goes Wrong

With a ZZ Plant, more water isn't usually the answer.

These plants are built to handle dry periods, so start by checking the soil and roots before reaching for the watering can.

01

Yellow leaves

Yellowing can be a sign of overly wet soil, especially when the plant is watered too often.

→ 02

Soft or mushy stems

Soft stems can point to excess moisture or problems with the roots.

→ 03

Slow growth

ZZ Plants naturally grow at a slower pace, and lower light can slow them down even further.
