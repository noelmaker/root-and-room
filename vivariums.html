HOW LIVING HABITATS WORK

Different forms of life. One connected habitat.

A bioactive vivarium brings animals, plants, microorganisms, and natural materials together in a carefully managed environment. Each component plays a role, but the needs of the animal inhabitants always come first.

01

Animal Inhabitants

Every habitat begins with its inhabitants. Their species-specific needs determine the appropriate temperature, humidity, lighting, ventilation, and space required for a healthy environment.

02

Plants & Natural Structures

Suitable living plants, branches, cork bark, and natural hiding places create a more enriching environment. These elements provide shelter and opportunities for natural behaviors while helping recreate aspects of the animal's habitat.

03

Cleanup Crews & Living Soil

In suitable bioactive setups, springtails, isopods, and beneficial microorganisms help break down organic waste and recycle nutrients. A properly chosen substrate supports this process, although regular cleaning and habitat maintenance are still necessary.

Three connected components. One thoughtfully designed habitat.

WHERE EVERY HABITAT BEGINS

A thriving habitat begins with understanding.

Building a vivarium is about more than creating a beautiful landscape. It's about designing an environment where its inhabitants can feel secure, express natural behaviors, and thrive. Here's where to begin.

01
RESEARCH FIRST

Understand Your Inhabitants

Start by researching the animal species you plan to keep. Learn about its adult size, temperature and humidity needs, lighting requirements, natural behaviors, and preferred habitat. These details will guide every decision that follows.

↗
02
PLAN YOUR ENVIRONMENT

Design Your Living Habitat

Choose an appropriately sized enclosure and plan its heating, lighting, ventilation, and moisture management. Add suitable substrate, safe plants, climbing structures, and hiding places based on the needs of your chosen species.

↗
03
ESTABLISH & MAINTAIN

Build, Monitor & Maintain

Assemble your habitat and establish stable environmental conditions before introducing animals. Where appropriate, allow plants and cleanup crews time to establish. Continue monitoring temperature, humidity, cleanliness, and animal behavior over time.

↗

Research thoughtfully. Build patiently. Let life thrive.
