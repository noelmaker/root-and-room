Explore the Armadillidium genus.

From the familiar Common Pill Bug to the striking patterns of Zebra and Clown isopods, Armadillidium includes a remarkable diversity of terrestrial isopods. Explore their distinctive appearances, natural habitats, behaviors, and popular hobby varieties.

01 / ARMADILLIDIUM VULGARE

Armadillidium vulgare

Commonly known as the Common Pill Bug, Armadillidium vulgare is a widespread terrestrial isopod recognized for its rounded, armored body and ability to roll into a protective ball. Naturally occurring in a range of muted colors and patterns, this adaptable species is also available in several selectively bred varieties popular among hobbyists.

POPULAR HOBBY VARIETIES
Magic Potion Orange Vigor T+ Albino Punta Cana
02 / ARMADILLIDIUM MACULATUM

Armadillidium maculatum

Commonly known as the Zebra Isopod, Armadillidium maculatum is recognized for its striking light-colored bands against a dark, segmented body. Native to parts of the Mediterranean region, this species is a favorite among hobbyists for its distinctive appearance, relatively straightforward care, and selectively bred color varieties.

POPULAR HOBBY VARIETIES
Classic Zebra High White Yellow Zebra Chocolate Zebra
03 / ARMADILLIDIUM KLUGII

Armadillidium klugii

Commonly known as the Clown Isopod, Armadillidium klugii is admired for its striking rows of pale spots against a dark body, often accented by reddish or orange coloration. Native to parts of the western Balkans, this species is popular among collectors for its distinctive patterns and several recognizable geographic forms.

POPULAR HOBBY VARIETIES
Montenegro Dubrovnik Pudding Orange
04 / ARMADILLIDIUM GESTROI

Armadillidium gestroi

Armadillidium gestroi is a striking terrestrial isopod native to parts of Italy, particularly the island of Sardinia. Recognized for its bold yellow markings against a dark, glossy body, this species is popular among hobbyists for its impressive appearance and relatively large size compared with many other Armadillidium species.

DISTINCTIVE CHARACTERISTICS
Yellow Markings Dark Glossy Body Large Size Full Conglobation
05 / ARMADILLIDIUM GRANULATUM

Armadillidium granulatum

Armadillidium granulatum is a Mediterranean terrestrial isopod recognized for its distinctly textured exoskeleton, covered in small raised granules. Its muted gray and brown coloration helps it blend into rocky environments and leaf litter. Popular among collectors for its rugged appearance, this species offers an interesting contrast to the smoother-bodied members of the genus.

DISTINCTIVE CHARACTERISTICS
Granular Exoskeleton Earth-Toned Coloration Mediterranean Species Full Conglobation
06 / ARMADILLIDIUM NASATUM

Armadillidium nasatum

Commonly known as the Nosy Pill Woodlouse, Armadillidium nasatum is recognized by the small, nose-like projection extending from the front of its head. Often found in gardens, leaf litter, and other sheltered environments, this species typically displays muted gray or brown coloration. Unlike many of its relatives, it may form a slightly imperfect ball when curling up for protection.

DISTINCTIVE CHARACTERISTICS
Nose-Like Projection Gray & Brown Tones Garden Dweller Imperfect Conglobation
07 / ARMADILLIDIUM PERACCAE

Armadillidium peraccae

Armadillidium peraccae is a terrestrial isopod native to parts of the Mediterranean region. Known for its textured exoskeleton and subdued gray-brown coloration, this species has a rugged appearance that distinguishes it from smoother-bodied Armadillidium. Its relatively understated coloring makes it an interesting choice for hobbyists who appreciate natural patterns and subtle variations.

DISTINCTIVE CHARACTERISTICS
Textured Exoskeleton Earth-Toned Coloration Mediterranean Origin Natural Patterns
08 / ARMADILLIDIUM VERSICOLOR

Armadillidium versicolor

Armadillidium versicolor is a terrestrial isopod native to parts of southeastern Europe. Known for its variable coloration and patterned exoskeleton, individuals may display combinations of gray, brown, and lighter markings. Its naturally diverse appearance makes this species an interesting addition for collectors who appreciate subtle differences in color and pattern.

DISTINCTIVE CHARACTERISTICS
Variable Coloration Natural Patterns European Species Full Conglobation
