Plant Library

Peace Lily

An elegant tropical houseplant loved for its glossy green leaves, graceful white blooms, and ability to thrive in indirect light.

Light Medium to bright indirect
Water When the top inch feels dry
Care Easy to moderate
Pet safety Toxic to cats and dogs

Before You Buy

Is this plant right for your home?

Peace Lilies bring a soft, tropical feel to indoor spaces with their glossy green leaves and elegant white blooms. They're relatively easy to care for, but prefer consistent moisture and protection from direct sunlight.

Good match if...

You want a beautiful foliage plant with occasional white blooms, have a spot with indirect light, and don't mind checking the soil regularly.

Think twice if...

You frequently forget to water your plants, have pets that may chew on leaves, or only have a spot that receives harsh direct sunlight.

Worth knowing

Peace Lilies may dramatically droop when thirsty, but often perk up after watering. They can tolerate lower light, although brighter indirect light generally encourages more blooms.

Care Basics

A little consistency goes a long way.

Peace Lilies thrive with a simple care routine. Keep an eye on soil moisture, provide gentle indirect light, and remove aging leaves to keep your plant looking its best.

01

Keep the soil lightly moist

Water when the top inch of soil feels dry. Water thoroughly, allowing excess water to drain away. Avoid leaving the roots sitting in soggy soil.

02

Choose gentle, indirect light

Place your Peace Lily in medium to bright indirect light. It can tolerate lower-light conditions, but may produce fewer blooms. Avoid prolonged direct afternoon sunlight, which can scorch the leaves.

03

Remove faded blooms and leaves

Trim spent flower stalks near their base and remove yellow or damaged leaves using clean scissors. Peace Lilies generally don't need frequent pruning to maintain their shape.

What Usually Goes Wrong

When your Peace Lily needs a little attention.

Peace Lilies are expressive plants, and changes in their leaves can offer clues about their growing conditions. Here's what to check when something seems off.

01

Yellow leaves

Yellowing can result from overwatering, natural aging, or other growing conditions. Check the soil moisture and drainage first.

02

Drooping leaves

Peace Lilies often droop when thirsty, but consistently wet soil can cause similar symptoms. Feel the soil before reaching for your watering can.

03

Brown leaf tips

Brown tips may develop from dry air, inconsistent watering, or mineral and salt buildup in the soil. Review your watering routine and water quality.
