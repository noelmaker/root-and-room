Spider Plant

An adaptable houseplant with graceful arching leaves, easygoing care, and plenty of personality as it grows.

Light Bright to low
Water Let soil dry slightly between watering
Care Moderate attention
Pet safety Generally considered pet-friendly

Before You Buy

Is this plant right for your home?

Spider Plants are adaptable and generally forgiving, but a few details can help you decide whether they're a good fit for your space and routine.

Good match if...

You want an adaptable plant that can handle a range of indoor conditions and doesn't need constant attention.

Think twice if...

You want a plant that stays compact or you're looking for something with a more dramatic, upright shape.

Worth knowing

Spider Plants can produce long runners and small plantlets, giving them a naturally full, trailing look.

Care Basics

Give it what it needs, then let it grow.

Spider Plants are adaptable and relatively easygoing. A few consistent habits will help them stay healthy and produce their characteristic plantlets.

01

Let the soil dry slightly

Allow the top portion of the soil to dry before watering again rather than keeping it constantly wet.

02

Give it bright, indirect light

Spider Plants can adapt to lower light, but brighter indirect light can support fuller growth.

03

Watch for dry conditions

Curling or browning leaf tips can be a sign that the plant is getting too dry or receiving harsh light.

What Usually Goes Wrong

When the leaves look unhappy, start here.

Spider Plants are forgiving, but changes in watering, light, or growing conditions can show up quickly in their leaves.

01

Brown leaf tips

Dry soil, inconsistent watering, or dry indoor air can contribute to brown tips.

→ 02

Yellow leaves

Check the soil first. Consistently wet conditions can cause problems with the roots.

→ 03

Faded or pale leaves

Harsh direct sunlight can cause fading or scorching, while very low light can reduce vigorous growth.
