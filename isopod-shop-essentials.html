THE FINISHING TOUCHES

The little details make a world of difference.

A thriving isopod habitat is more than substrate and an enclosure. From natural hiding places to calcium sources and everyday care tools, these thoughtful additions help create a comfortable home for your colony.

01 / SHELTER & SECURITY

Cork Bark & Natural Hides

Isopods spend much of their time tucked beneath bark and other natural cover. Cork bark creates sheltered spaces where colonies can gather, explore, and retreat from light and disturbance.

BEST FOR

Providing secure hiding places, additional climbing surfaces, and a more natural habitat environment.

KEEP IN MIND

Choose untreated, pesticide-free cork bark. Place hides across different moisture zones so isopods can choose where they feel most comfortable.

02 / HEALTH & GROWTH

Calcium & Mineral Sources

Calcium plays an important role in building and maintaining an isopod's exoskeleton. Natural sources such as cuttlebone and calcium carbonate can help supplement the minerals available in their habitat.

BEST FOR

Supporting healthy exoskeleton development, molting, and the long-term nutritional needs of your colony.

KEEP IN MIND

Use plain, additive-free calcium sources. Avoid flavored cuttlebone or mineral products containing unnecessary ingredients.

03 / EVERYDAY CARE

Feeding & Maintenance Tools

A few simple tools can make colony care easier. Shallow feeding dishes, pipettes, spray bottles, and soft brushes help you manage moisture, offer supplemental foods, and maintain your enclosure with less disruption.

BEST FOR

Making feeding, moisture management, and routine habitat maintenance more convenient.

KEEP IN MIND

Choose easy-to-clean tools and avoid spraying isopods directly. Remove uneaten supplemental foods before they spoil or attract unwanted pests.

BEFORE YOU BUY

Thoughtful details. Happier little habitats.

The best habitat accessories are simple, safe, and practical. Choosing the right materials and tools helps you care for your isopods while keeping their environment comfortable and easy to maintain.

01

Choose Natural, Untreated Materials

Look for pesticide-free cork bark and decorations without paints, fragrances, or chemical treatments. Natural materials provide useful hiding places without introducing unnecessary substances into the habitat.

02

Keep Calcium Available

Isopods use calcium to build and maintain their exoskeletons. Plain cuttlebone, suitable calcium carbonate, or other appropriate mineral sources can supplement the calcium already present in their diet and substrate.

03

Keep Feeding Simple

Small, shallow feeding dishes can help keep supplemental foods separate from the substrate. Offer manageable portions and remove uneaten perishables before they spoil or attract unwanted pests.

04

Use Gentle Maintenance Tools

Pipettes, fine-mist spray bottles, and soft brushes can make routine care easier. Add water carefully, avoid spraying isopods directly, and check for tiny juveniles before moving decorations or cleaning the enclosure.
